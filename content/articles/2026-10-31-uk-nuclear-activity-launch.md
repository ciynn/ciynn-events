[Read the full issue (PDF)](https://npg.dl.ac.uk/OutreachNewsletter/Newsletters/2026/NPActivity_October2026.pdf), or browse the [newsletter archive](https://npg.dl.ac.uk/OutreachNewsletter/index.html).*

PhD researchers at the University of Liverpool have founded the Chadwick Institute Young Nuclear Network (CIYNN). It is a student-led community that connects students, postdocs and other early-career researchers in nuclear physics and related fields.

The network starts at a time of fast growth. New UKRI doctoral programmes, funded through a £65.6M nuclear skills call and match-funded by industry, started in 2026. For example, RAPTOR (Radiation Protection, Nuclear Safety and Environmental Sustainability) is led by the University of Liverpool, working with Manchester's Dalton Nuclear Institute and the universities of Surrey, Suffolk and Lancaster. RAPTOR will train 80 doctoral researchers alongside partners from the nuclear energy and security sectors, including the radiation-detection company Mirion Technologies, which is supporting three studentships. SATURN 2 brings together seven universities, including the Universities of Liverpool and Manchester, and 22 industry partners, and will train around 50 PhD and EngD students a year.

CIYNN aims to give early-career researchers and students a share in that collaboration. The network is based in Liverpool's Chadwick Institute. It aims to build links between universities, so that young people can share ideas, skills and opportunities beyond their group. It is also working with professional membership bodies and with industry, giving members a clearer view of careers in and beyond academia and a direct line to the organisations that shape the field.

"Networks like this are vital for strengthening collaboration between academia and industry, and for building innovation and leadership in the next generation of nuclear professionals," says Marcus Turner-Wood, PhD student in Liverpool's Nuclear Physics Cluster.

The network welcomes early-career researchers from any UK institution, as well as organisations interested in working with it. To get involved, [join the network](../join.html) or email ChadwickInstituteNetwork@outlook.com.
